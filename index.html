Technology Selection

Which Process Is Right
For Your Project?

Not sure whether to use 3D printing or CNC machining? Our hybrid approach lets you combine both for the best outcome.

Your Requirement Recommended Process Why
Complex geometry / Internal cooling channels 3D Printing No tool access limitations — can produce any internal geometry
High precision / Metal / Superior surface finish CNC Machining Tolerance up to ±0.01mm with Ra 0.8 surface finish achievable
Rapid prototype + Subsequent mass production Hybrid 3D print for validation → CNC for production, seamless iteration
Low-volume production (<200 units) Hybrid No mold tooling cost — ideal for small batches
Functional testing / Rapid design iteration 3D Printing Same-day printing possible, iterate design in hours not weeks
Aerospace / Medical certified materials CNC Machining Full traceability, material certs (EN10204, AS9100 docs)

Core Differentiator

Hybrid Manufacturing Solutions

The real power: combining both technologies. We engineer the right sequence for each project's unique requirements.

01

3D Print + CNC Finish

Print complex internal geometries with 3D printing, then CNC-machine precision surfaces and critical features in one setup.

Eliminates tool access limitations
Same material from print to CNC
Ideal for conformal cooling channels
02

Prototype → Mass Production

Verify your design with 3D printed prototypes, iterate fast, then transition to CNC-machined production parts — no new tooling required.

Zero mold tooling cost for low volumes
Design validated before CNC production
Scale from 1 to 1000+ units seamlessly
03

3D Printed Fixture + CNC

Print custom jigs, fixtures, and gauge blocks at a fraction of machined cost, then use them to improve CNC setup accuracy and speed.

Cut fixture costs by up to 80%
Reduce CNC setup time significantly
Custom geometries impossible to machine

Titanium Exhaust Flange — SLM + CNC Post-Machining

A US aftermarket exhaust brand needed 30 Ti-6Al-4V flanges with internal flow-optimized geometry impossible to machine from solid. Printed via SLM, then 5-axis CNC finished mounting surfaces and bolt holes to ±0.01mm tolerance.

German robotics company required 80 A356-T6 joint housings with complex internal channels for cable routing. Sand-cast blanks produced in 5 days, then CNC-machined bearing seats and mating faces to Ra 0.8 finish.
